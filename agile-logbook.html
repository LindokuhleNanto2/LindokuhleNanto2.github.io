Evidence 05 · Progress over time

Agile Logbook

This logbook brings together the work records from July, August and September 2026 to show how BrightStart moved from the initial business idea toward a working website.

Entries summarise the supplied project records; they do not imply that every planned task was completed on the same day.

July 2026 · Business idea and presentation

Tuesday, 14 July

Discussed the business idea

The team discussed the final business idea and began preparing the PowerPoint business plan. This work helped turn the concept into something that could be explained and evaluated.

Wednesday, 15 July

Reviewed the business presentation

The team reviewed the PowerPoint and made minor changes to improve the presentation before presenting it.

Thursday, 16 July

Rehearsal and feedback

The presentation was rehearsed and practised, with feedback considered before the final presentation.

Friday, 17 July

Presented BrightStart

The team presented the BrightStart business idea to the lecturer. The next phase was to move from explaining the concept toward building the project.

August 2026 · Organising design and development

Tuesday, 4 August

Updated Trello and planned the next tasks

The Trello board was reviewed and dates were added. Planned work included completing the board, preparing the lecturer presentation and planning the frontend. Team availability was a blocker.

Wednesday, 5 August

Created the brand guide

The brand guide was created and presented to the lecturer. The next tasks were to continue frontend work and start the Business Model. One team member was absent due to illness.

Thursday, 6 August

Set up GitHub and assigned development tasks

The repository was set up, development tasks were assigned and the frontend was planned around the approved Figma design. The team planned to finish Trello organisation and continue frontend development.

Friday, 7 August

Updated the board and started Business Architecture

Trello was edited based on feedback and Business Architecture work began. The next focus was to complete the frontend, move toward functionality and start backend work. No blocker was recorded for this day.

August · Ongoing taskboard work

Requirements, stories and project setup

The taskboard work included defining functional requirements, assigning tasks and timelines, organising sprint-labelled groups, adding user stories and dates, creating the brand guide, preparing for presentations, reviewing the frontend prototype and maintaining the GitHub repository. Accessing or preparing the VS Code project caused difficulty, so some work continued using the prototype while the project setup was organised.

September 2026 · Integration, testing and final demonstration

September · Design and integration

Finalised the frontend and combined components

The team worked on the final frontend design and presented it. The frontend and backend were combined, and checks were made to see whether they worked together.

September · Database connection

Connected the database to the website

The database was created and connected to the website. This moved the project beyond static screen design and toward a system that could work with stored data.

September · Final demonstration

Presented the functional website

The team presented the final functional website. Testing and integration were important because the individual frontend screens had to operate correctly with backend services and data.

My individual contribution and learning

Across the project, my main contribution was design and user experience. I created Figma screen designs and worked on the login and account-creation screens. During implementation, I also helped address the payment error and helped test frontend–backend integration.

My reflection

Looking back at the logbook, I can see the project developing in stages. In July, the focus was on explaining and presenting the idea. In August, more attention went to the brand guide, Figma/prototype, Trello organisation, requirements and development setup. In September, the work shifted toward combining components, connecting the database and demonstrating the working website.

The experience taught me that project progress is not always a straight line. Team availability and setup problems affected some planned work, and the team had to keep communicating and adjusting. My UI/UX work gave me experience turning requirements into screens, but helping with the payment error and integration testing also showed me the importance of checking the complete user journey. A design can look correct while a real action still fails because of data, authentication, storage or backend behaviour. I now understand why testing should happen throughout development rather than only at the end.

If I repeated the project, I would keep a clearer record of what was completed each day, what was carried forward, who owned each task and what evidence proved it worked. That would make the logbook and Trello board even more useful for reflecting on progress and explaining decisions.
