Evidence 04 · Trello workflow

Trello Taskboard and Sprint Backlog

Trello was the team’s project-management tool. The board used dated groups labelled Sprint 1 onward to organise work, while the charter described a Kanban-style flow for tracking progress.

Tool used: Trello only · Workflow agreed in the team charter: To Do → In Progress → Testing → Done

How the board supported delivery

The board began with project understanding and team setup, then moved through research, business planning, requirements, design, implementation and testing. Sprint labels and dates helped the team see when work was intended to happen. The charter also expected members to keep cards updated, identify blockers early and move tasks as progress changed.

Work represented on the Trello board

Early planning · Sprint 1–2

Understand the problem and organise the team

Review the project brief, form the team, define roles, brainstorm and select a business idea, define community context and problem statement, and conduct market research.

Research and business definition · Sprint 3–5

Validate and refine BrightStart

Collect and analyse survey responses, present the initial idea, review lecturer feedback, refine and finalise the idea, create wireframes, prepare the business proposal and create the initial product backlog.

Requirements and planning · Sprint 6–8

Define the product to build

Set up Trello, refine the business idea, complete the 5Ws, conduct feasibility analysis, define the MVP, identify stakeholders, gather system requirements, define system features, prioritise the backlog and divide it into sprint-labelled work.

Repository and implementation setup · Sprint 8–9

Prepare the development environment

Review wireframes, prepare the presentation, set up GitHub and the project structure, and plan website navigation and page implementation.

Website build · later board groups

Design and implement the website

Develop the Home, About Us, Services, Registration, Quiz, Progress and Contact pages; add form validation and quiz functionality; apply styling and responsive layouts; and integrate website components.

Quality and delivery · final board groups

Test, refine and prepare the final submission

Perform initial testing; test navigation, registration and quiz behaviour; fix bugs; improve UI and responsive behaviour; prepare documentation; review the Agile Logbook and Trello board; merge GitHub branches; deploy; conduct final system testing and present the result.

My contribution within this workflow

My main role was UI/UX Designer. I created Figma screen designs and worked on the login and account-creation screens. During implementation, I also helped troubleshoot the payment error and assisted with checking whether the frontend integrated correctly with the backend. This work connected the design phase with the technical testing and refinement stages.

Blockers and adjustments

In the August work records, team availability was an impediment at times, including one member being unavailable and another absent due to illness. There were also difficulties accessing or preparing the VS Code project. The team continued organising Trello, preparing the brand guide and user stories, and using the prototype while project setup and development work continued. These are recorded as practical constraints, not as evidence that every task was completed on its original date.

My reflection

Trello helped make a large project feel more manageable by breaking it into visible tasks. I could see that design was one part of a wider workflow: research informed the requirements, requirements informed the screens, and testing helped confirm whether the screens worked with the system. The dated groups gave the team a way to organise intended work, while card updates were important for communicating what had actually progressed.

The project also showed me that a plan needs to adapt. Team availability and setup difficulties can affect what is possible in a given week. Rather than treating the board as a document that is completed once, it needs to reflect the current state of work. I learned to value communication, realistic planning and early testing. In future, I would flag integration tasks and their dependencies more explicitly, because a payment issue can involve multiple components and cannot always be resolved by changing the interface alone.

Evidence source: Trello board, 27 October 2026; team charter; July–September work records supplied for this portfolio.
