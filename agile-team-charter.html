Agile Team Charter

The charter gave the BrightStart team a shared purpose, clear responsibilities and a practical agreement about how we would work together.

Team charter last updated 4 May 2026 · Framework: Kanban managed in Trello

Team purpose and product vision

BrightStart aims to support English literacy for young learners in South Africa, particularly Grades 1–3. The team identified reading, writing and comprehension as important learning areas because difficulties with foundational English can affect learners’ confidence and their ability to understand other subjects as they progress through school.

The planned service is a web-based English literacy learning platform with reading exercises, writing practice, simple quizzes and progress tracking. The intended result is a functional, user-friendly website that supports learners and gives parents or tutors a way to follow progress.

How we worked

  • Workflow: Trello was used as the team's taskboard with a Kanban-style flow: To Do, In Progress, Testing and Done.
  • Communication: Microsoft Teams and WhatsApp for team communication, Trello for task progress, and email for formal documents.
  • Planning and review: The charter planned regular check-ins, planning when needed, reviews or demonstrations at milestones, and retrospectives to identify improvements.
  • Decision-making: The team aimed for consensus, with the Product Owner making final decisions where project vision or priorities were affected.

Definition of Done

A task was intended to be considered complete when its design and implementation were finished, the interface was consistent with Figma, functionality had been tested and major issues addressed, the Trello card was updated, documentation was complete, requirements were met, and the work was ready for review or demonstration.

Risks, dependencies and constraints

The charter identified poor time management, communication or participation issues, technical difficulties and limited experience with design or coding tools as risks. Progress also depended on internet access, team availability and lecturer feedback. Academic deadlines and the team’s available skills and tools were constraints that needed to be managed.

My reflection

Having the charter helped me understand how my UI/UX responsibilities fitted into the wider project. I was responsible for designing screens and thinking about how users would move through the website, but the design could not be treated as separate from development. The Definition of Done reminded me that a screen is not complete simply because it looks good: it must also work, be tested, match the intended experience and be ready for review.

The charter also made communication and accountability more visible. When team members were unavailable or technical problems occurred, the work could be affected even if an individual had completed their own task. I learned that raising blockers early, updating Trello and checking in with teammates are part of delivering a reliable product, not just administrative work.
