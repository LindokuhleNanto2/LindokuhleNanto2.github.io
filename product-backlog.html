Evidence 03 · Product scope and priorities

Product Backlog

The product backlog groups the work needed to make BrightStart useful to learners, parents, tutors and administrators. It connects the learning purpose to the account, booking, payment and progress functions.

Priorities below are a reasoned grouping of documented project features, not a claim of exact Trello card priority values.

Product goal

Deliver a usable web-based English tutoring service that supports learners in Grades 1–3 and allows parents or guardians to register, book sessions and monitor progress, while tutors and administrators manage learning and service operations.

How this connects to the Trello board

The Trello board records early planning and research tasks, including reviewing the brief, forming the team, defining the problem, conducting market research, analysing survey results and presenting the business idea. It then moves into product and requirements work such as creating wireframes, preparing the proposal, defining the MVP, gathering system requirements, defining features, prioritising the backlog and dividing work into sprint-labelled groups.

Later board tasks include setting up GitHub and the project structure, designing navigation and pages, developing registration and quiz functionality, applying styling, integrating components, testing, fixing bugs, improving the UI, merging branches, deployment, final system testing and presentation. These task groups show the progression from understanding the problem to designing, building and checking the solution.

My reflection

The backlog helped me see how UI work depends on decisions made earlier in the project. The team needed to understand the learners and service first, then define the features, and only then could the screens and user flows be designed around them. As I created the Figma screens and worked on login and account creation, I could connect visual decisions to actual user needs rather than designing screens in isolation.

I also learned that the backlog is not just a list of features. Integration, testing, error fixing and documentation need to be included because they affect whether the product is usable. Our payment issue was a practical example: a screen could appear complete while the workflow still needed troubleshooting between the frontend and backend. In future, I would make the acceptance checks for high-risk flows such as registration, booking and payment visible earlier, so the team can test them before the final stage.
