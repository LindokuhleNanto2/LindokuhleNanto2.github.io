Personal reflection · Closing statement

What I learned as a UI/UX Designer

BrightStart gave me an opportunity to contribute to a real team project from the early idea and design stages through implementation support, integration testing and presentation.

Lindokuhle Nanto · UI/UX Designer

My role and contribution

My assigned role in the Agile Team Charter was UI/UX Designer. I created Figma screen designs and helped plan the layout and experience of the website. I made the login and account-creation screens, which are important because users need a clear way to enter the service and create an account before they can use the relevant features.

My contribution was not limited to design. When we experienced a payment error, I helped with troubleshooting it. I also helped test whether the frontend and backend integrated correctly. This gave me a better understanding of the connection between what users see on the screen and what the system needs to do behind the scenes.

What the portfolio evidence shows

Planning and teamwork

The charter and Trello taskboard show how the team agreed on responsibilities and organised work. The logbook records how planning changed as the project progressed.

User-centred design

The Figma work and login/account-creation screens reflect the need to make important user journeys understandable and consistent.

Problem-solving

Helping troubleshoot the payment error showed me that a feature may require collaboration across the interface and backend, rather than a visual change alone.

Testing and integration

Checking the frontend with the backend helped me see why screens must be tested using real system behaviour and saved data.

My personal reflection

One of my biggest lessons from BrightStart is that UI/UX design is not only about making a website attractive. It is about understanding what a user wants to achieve, arranging information clearly and helping the user know what to do next. Designing the login and account-creation screens in Figma encouraged me to think about clarity and consistency, especially for users who may not be confident with technology.

I also learned that design and development are connected. When the payment error occurred, it was important to help investigate the problem and test the flow instead of assuming the screen was finished. Working with the frontend and backend helped me appreciate how data, status updates and error messages affect the user's experience. A user does not see the separate layers of a system; they experience the website as one service, so those parts need to work together.

Teamwork and communication were also important. Trello helped the team organise tasks, but the board only stayed useful when work was updated and blockers were communicated. Team availability and technical setup challenges reminded me that plans sometimes need to change. I learned to be more aware of dependencies and to ask whether a feature has been tested from beginning to end, rather than judging progress only by whether a screen has been designed.

In future projects, I want to strengthen my ability to test user journeys, document design decisions and collaborate with developers earlier. I would also encourage the team to test high-risk flows such as account creation, booking and payment as soon as an initial version is available. Overall, BrightStart helped me connect user-centred design with practical software development and gave me more confidence contributing beyond my assigned design tasks.

My main takeaway: a successful interface is not just one that looks right; it must help the user complete the task and work reliably with the system behind it.

Conclusion

BrightStart brought together business planning, requirements, interface design, development, integration and testing. The portfolio evidence demonstrates the work process as well as the lessons I learned. My contribution as UI/UX Designer was strongest in Figma and the login/account-creation screens, and I also supported payment troubleshooting and frontend–backend testing. These experiences helped me understand the value of a collaborative, user-focused approach to building a functional website.
